# THE OLD FOREST

## The Yggdrasil, the Holzweiber, the Dryad and the Kodama: four classes from before the Garden

A worldbuilding note, not a class document. Figures follow from the stated particulars; reaches marked *est.* are rough estimates and remain to be rated under TORMENTA.

---

## 1. Two plans

The ships of ARCVS Void Command are built under long procurement plans. The current one is the **Garden Procurement and Logistics Plan**, which produced the PAPILION, PHALAENA, AESHNA and their auxiliaries.

Before the Garden there was the **Mystical Forest Plan**, now called the **Old Forest Plan**. Its ships were named for the forest and its spirits: Yggdrasil, the world tree; the Holzweiber, the wood-wives of German folklore; the Dryad, a tree nymph; and the Kodama, the tree spirits of Japan. The Garden plan names its ships for what lives in the garden.

All four forest classes are still in existence, in large numbers, on top of everything the Garden plan has built:

| Class | Built as | Serves now as | Numbers |
|---|---|---|---|
| Yggdrasil | ship of the line | light cruiser; internal escort; sold to principalities and commonwealths | ~8,000 |
| Holzweiber | third-rate cruiser | picket and light escort; active ranging for frigates; screening auxiliaries | ~22,000 built, most in service |
| Dryad | experimental heavy frigate | local police frigate, internal picket, training ship, patrol | ~40,000 |
| Kodama | standard light frigate, or corvette | local police frigate; depot training hull; in ordinary | ~54,000, about 30% in service |

The Dryad is the only class to have entered two plans. She was built under the Old Forest Plan and carried on into the Garden. The Kodama is the only class held in ordinary.

### 1.1 Gradients across the Old Forest

| Class | Role | Length | Citadel | Gradient | Particle energy |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Kodama | light frigate, corvette | 170 m | 120 m | 50 MeV/m | 6 GeV |
| Dryad | heavy frigate | 200 m | 150 m | 70 MeV/m | 10 GeV |
| Holzweiber | third-rate cruiser | 300 m | 150 m | 80 MeV/m | 12 GeV |
| Yggdrasil | ship of the line | 350 m | ~178 m accelerating | 90 MeV/m | 16 GeV |
| Garden plan | all classes | | | 100 MeV/m | |

Gradient rose with hull size everywhere except the Dryad, which jumped 20 MeV/m past the Kodama in a hull only 30 m longer. That is what makes her revolutionary.

### 1.2 Three experiments

The Old Forest Plan tried three ideas the Garden kept:

- **The Yggdrasil's domes** became the AESHNA's domes.
- **The Holzweiber's long strips** became the wing-edge strips of the PAPILION and PHALAENA.
- **The Dryad's thin channels** became the FILVM thread.

She delivers a little under half a PAPILION's beam energy per salvo, with a beam less stiff against the field. One Yggdrasil stack probably reaches about 60–75% of a PAPILION stack: on the order of 2,200–2,700 km against a woven hull, and 7,000–8,500 km for de-masting, *est.* She fights a titanium navy well and is outranged by a PAPILION in every band. Her lighter charge per stack is the other side of that: less load on the mane for each salvo.

### 2.3 The domes

Her optics sit in two blister domes on the hull, level with its plane, rather than on wing edges. Her radiators were not shaped as wings with deliberate leading edges, by doctrine and by the taste of her era. Dedicated strips cover the horizon, where a level dome is weakest.

| | Hull domes (Yggdrasil) | Wing edges (Garden plan) |
|---|---|---|
| Coverage | all round | strongest forward, aimed by heading |
| Point defence | good on every bearing | weaker off the bow |
| Baseline for a multi-station solve | short: the domes sit on the hull | long: 140 m between forewings |
| Thermal path to the radiators | longer | short |
| Distance from the spine | close | well off the axis |

Domes suit defence; wing edges suit seeing and aiming forward. The Garden plan put its line ships' and frigates' optics on wing edges, then brought domes back for the one class whose first duty is point defence. The AESHNA's five domes, each with its own offset apex and cant, read as the Yggdrasil's two level domes refined. Two domes and strips worked. Five domes proved better.

### 2.4 The guns

Her main battery is the KGV layout. The King George V class battleships of 1939–40 carried a quadruple turret with a twin superfiring over it. The Yggdrasil carries that group dorsal and ventral, so her whole broadside is balanced by design, and it equals a PAPILION's balanced broadside.

The KGV's quadruple turrets were notoriously unreliable in action. The ARCVS experience was the reverse. The kinetic battery was so reliable, so efficient and so well mastered that the Garden plan carried it straight into the PAPILION (§6.3).

### 2.5 Her service now

She is no longer a ship of the line. She serves as a light cruiser: still battle-worthy, and still a good ship.

Her most important duty is internal escort. The AESHNA is hard to build. Every dome is unique, a yard holds spare positions rather than spare domes, and each loomyard building AESHNA is not building PAPILION or PHALAENA. Internal escort faces low threats, so the Yggdrasil fills it well enough, and the scarce ships and yard capacity go forward.

She is also sold to ARCVS principalities and commonwealths. A capable heavy ship the AVC is replacing anyway is the natural hull for an allied navy to buy.

### 2.6 Analogues

- **The 50-gun two-decker.** Too weak for the battle line by the late eighteenth century, and too strong for anything smaller. Navies used them for decades as flagships on distant stations, including Newfoundland, and as convoy leaders.
- **The Atlanta class.** American anti-aircraft cruisers of the Second World War, valued for defending a formation rather than fighting other cruisers.
- **The Ticonderoga class and Aegis.** Cruisers defined by a phased-array defence system that protects a whole formation, rated as cruisers because of that role rather than their hull.
- **Destroyers for bases, 1940, and the Flower-class corvettes.** Obsolete or simple ships used as escorts while better ones were built. Good enough against the threat actually in front of them.
- **Long service.** HMS *Victory* was launched in 1765 and fought at Trafalgar forty years later. Ships of the line outlived the designs that replaced them.
- **The razee.** Sail navies cut down old two-deckers into heavy frigates. Pellew's *Indefatigable*, a 64 razeed in 1795, is the famous case. The Yggdrasil's demotion to cruiser is the same idea without the saw.

| | PHALAENA stack | Holzweiber stack | Yggdrasil stack |
|---|---|---|---|

| Current | 1.1 kA | 4.0 kA | 4.5 kA |

A Holzweiber stack carries nearly three times a PHALAENA stack's energy and about two-thirds of a Yggdrasil's. She hits far harder than any frigate she supports. That is why she can screen auxiliaries and back a picket line without being a true escort.

### 3.3 The long strips

She proved the idea of long strips. Hers run along her port and starboard sides, facing out across her beams.

The Garden plan kept the strip and turned it forward. On the PAPILION and PHALAENA the strips sit on wing leading edges, off the spinal axis, a short thermal path from the mane, and far enough apart to anchor a three-station solve. A strip facing along a ship's heading sees and aims where her spine aims. A strip facing her beam sees where her guns turn.

### 3.4 Two calibres, then one

As built she carried two natures: PILVM in her main turrets, and the 1.7 kg round in her secondary turrets. That put her in the pre-dreadnought tradition, with a main and a secondary battery on one hull.

Pre-dreadnoughts had a known problem with that. Splashes from different calibres were hard to tell apart, which spoiled fire correction, and HMS *Dreadnought* (1906) went to a single big-gun calibre partly for that reason. The Garden plan made the same choice in its own terms: one family of natures per class, one magazine and simpler tender loads.

Her only refit brought her into line with it. The two secondary turrets came out, and she now carries one nature.

### 3.5 Her service now: the emitter

In the current fleet she fits picket and light escort. She is not a true escort like the Yggdrasil, but she is worthy enough to support PHALAENA and to screen auxiliaries.

The refit is what makes her valuable there. Her two secondary turrets were replaced with active sensor blisters: not optical weapon domes, but active sensor work, ranging and tracking.

That gives her a job the PHALAENA cannot do well. In the frigate's own doctrine, ranging and picket are exclusive states, and a squadron ranging actively is a squadron that has been located. A Holzweiber can do the active ranging and tracking while the PHALAENA beside her stay cold. She takes on the cost of being seen so that the frigates do not have to.

### 3.6 The SALTICIDAE after her

In function she is a scaled-up SALTICIDAE VIGIL: a hull whose value lies in seeing, tracking and passing on what she sees. The Vigil may well be the ship the Garden plan built after her: the eye kept, the battle-line hull, spine and guns left behind, at 18 kt instead of a cruiser's displacement. The Holzweiber is the watcher that can still fight. The SALTICIDAE is the watcher built only to watch.

### 3.7 Analogues

- **The third rate.** In sail, the third rate, the 74, was the backbone of the battle line: smaller than a first rate, and fully fit to fight in line. The Holzweiber is the cruiser of that description.
- **The pre-dreadnought.** Main and secondary batteries on one hull, until a single calibre proved simpler to fight.
- **Radar picket destroyers.** At Okinawa in 1945, the US Navy stationed destroyers far out from the fleet as radar pickets, emitting and spotting so the main force could be warned and directed. They drew the enemy's attention by doing it, and paid heavily.
- **Repeating frigates.** Sail fleets posted frigates outside the line to repeat the admiral's signals so every ship could read them. Their job was to be seen, so the line could act.

One Dryad stack probably reaches about half to two-thirds of a PHALAENA stack: masts on the order of 3,000–3,500 km, *est.* Her lower particle energy leaves her beam less stiff against the field, and that costs her more reach than the energy difference alone.

### 4.3 What she proved

She was built as the Old Forest Plan's experimental heavy frigate, and she became the backbone of modern ARCVS frigate doctrine.

Seventy MeV per metre was revolutionary on a ship her size. A higher gradient needs more power and sheds more heat per metre of accelerator, and a small hull has the least plant and radiator area to supply and cool it.

She was also the frigate that broke the 1 kA charge: the first to carry thin channels of around one kiloampere. She proved what the PHALAENA is built on, that many thin channels take eyes better than a few heavy ones. The FILVM thread is her descendant.

### 4.4 Her service now

- **Local police frigates.** She is the bulk of the frigates kept by member worlds' own police forces.
- **Internal picket formations.** Presence and observation inside safe space.
- **Patrol.**
- **Training.** She is the middle step of the Minerva pipeline. A Minerva is screened on cutters and tenders, learns a frigate's body on a Dryad, then goes to a PHALAENA. Fewer stacks and less charge per salvo make her a lighter load on a Minerva still learning to wear a frigate.

### 4.5 Analogues

- **The American heavy frigates.** Built under the Naval Act of 1794, larger and better armed than other frigates of their day, and serving across several eras of shipbuilding. USS *Constitution* is still in commission.
- **HMS *Dreadnought*.** The prototype that made the standard class second-line overnight. The Dryad did the same to the Kodama.
- **The training ship.** *Constitution* served as a training ship at the US Naval Academy in the 1860s, and Britain used the old first rate HMS *Britannia* to train cadets from 1859. Training on a proven, forgiving older ship is a long naval habit.
- **Old frigates on quiet stations.** Front-line years over, still the right ship for presence and policing where nothing modern is expected.

### 5.2 The standard the prototype replaced

She was the standard frigate of her plan. The Dryad was built beside her as the experimental heavy frigate, proved her ideas, and became the backbone of the doctrine that followed. The Kodama fitted her role decently, but that doctrine found no use for her in forward lines.

She is small for a warship, but she is not one of the current setting's cutters, and she fits neither the cutter's role nor a light escort's. She is too much a warship for a cutter's work: a spine is a short spear inside a planet's field, and she was built for open space. She is too weak for escort: a 6 GeV beam is stiff against nothing, and a Dryad does the same work better.

### 5.3 Among the police frigates

| | Kodama | Dryad | PHALAENA |
|---|---|---|---|
| Particle energy | 6 GeV | 10 GeV | 15 GeV |
| Gradient | 50 MeV/m | 70 MeV/m | 100 MeV/m |
| Length | 170 m | 200 m | 240 m |
| Police role | small worlds' approaches; the cheapest frigate | the bulk of police frigates; training | the best eyes, bought by worlds that can afford them |

On a small world's approaches, where the threat is a smuggler or a rogue hull, she is enough.

### 5.4 In ordinary

She is the only class held in ordinary. In sail, a ship laid up in ordinary was moored in harbour with her guns, stores and often her masts removed, but she was not abandoned. Her standing warrant officers, the boatswain, carpenter and gunner, usually stayed with her, keeping her sound so she could be fitted out again when war came. The Royal Navy mobilised from its ordinary again and again in the eighteenth century.

The ARCVS version may keep a Magister or two aboard: a Loom Master to keep the weave sound, perhaps a Mane Master.

The real limit on reactivating her is not the hulls but the Minervae. Bringing back 37,800 Kodama means finding 37,800 people to wear them. How fast cutters, tenders and Dryads can produce Minervae ready for a frigate sets how much of the ordinary is actually available in a great war. Her best wartime use is freeing better ships: a reactivated Kodama takes a world's approaches, and the Dryad or PHALAENA she replaces goes forward.

### 5.5 The training hull

The Kodama is not scrapped. She is not used for Minerva training, but she makes an excellent platform for loom tending and for learning it. Many military depots keep at least one, in ordinary, as a training vessel:

- **Damage control.** A depot cohort practises on a real hull of real weave rather than on mock-ups: real compartments, real breaches. The eleventh cohort, the one at depot reproducing the tradition, may well learn to save its own ship by first saving hers.
- **Re-weaving.** Loom yards learn the process on the actual material and the actual hull geometry, on a ship that can be cut and re-woven again and again.
- **Die cutting.** Die cutters practise their work permanently. A craft that is never finished being learned needs a hull that is never finished being worked.

She is cut and re-woven endlessly so that other ships can be healed in action: a woven hull keeping the promise of the weave over and over.

### 5.6 Analogues

- **The Cruizer-class brig-sloops.** The most numerous Royal Navy class of the Napoleonic era, more than a hundred built. Cheap, sound and everywhere, for patrol, convoy and the work no frigate could be spared for.
- **Ships in ordinary.** Laid up with a few warrant officers aboard, ready to fit out for war. In the twentieth century, the reserve or mothball fleets, such as the US Navy's after 1945.
- **HMS *Excellent*.** In 1830 the Royal Navy set up its gunnery school aboard an old ship moored in Portsmouth harbour. The name passed to later hulks and then to the shore establishment that grew from them.
- **HMS *Vernon*.** The torpedo school likewise began aboard old hulks before moving ashore.
- **Damage-control trainers.** Modern navies run flooding and fire trainers built as ship compartments. A Kodama is the real thing.

## 6. What the Garden plan changed, and what it kept

### 6.1 Two doctrines

| | Old Forest | Garden |
|---|---|---|
| The line | two ratings: Yggdrasil and Holzweiber | one: PAPILION, battlecruiser and ship of the line |
| Frigates | two experimental rates: Dryad, heavy, and Kodama, light | one: PHALAENA, the heavy frigate that won |
| Defence | domes on the line ship itself | a dedicated cruiser: AESHNA |
| Seeing and emitting | strips on a line ship: Holzweiber | a dedicated watcher: SALTICIDAE |
| Policing | frigates doing it on the side | a dedicated cutter: BACILLUS |

The Old Forest built generalists in graded sizes and gave each a little of everything. The Garden kept one line ship, supported by one heavy frigate, and moved everything else into specialist hulls. It traded variety of rating for variety of role.

Sail navies made the same consolidation. By the late eighteenth century the Royal Navy's line had two main ratings, the 74 and the smaller 64; the 64 came to be judged too weak for the line and was phased out, leaving the 74 as the standard. Frigates went the same way, as the older 28s gave way to heavier 32s, 36s and 38s. The Kodama is the 28; the Dryad is the 38.

The twentieth century repeated the line half. Navies built battleships for armour and gunpower and battlecruisers for speed, then merged them into the fast battleship, such as the American Iowa class. The PAPILION's double description, battlecruiser and ship of the line, is that merger.

### 6.2 Changed

- **The Minerva reforms.** The defining change of the Garden plan. A Minerva embodies a familiar shape far more readily than an unfamiliar one, and the Garden plan built its hulls as anatomy: wings with two positions, a mane felt rather than read, a forward truss that looks by facing. The Yggdrasil, with no wings and blister domes, was a harder body to wear. The garden is a garden of bodies a Minerva can inhabit.
- **Optics to the wing edges,** turning the Holzweiber's beam-facing strips forward, for non-collinearity, a short thermal path and the three-station solve. Domes returned only on the AESHNA, five of them, each offset and canted.
- **The gradient,** from 50, 70, 80 and 90 MeV/m to the fleet standard of 100.
- **Stack design.** More channels per stack on the line ship; four independent stacks on the frigate; the Mk.2 channel with half the energy spread.
- **One family of natures per class,** rather than the Holzweiber's two calibres. Her refit later brought her into line.

### 6.3 Kept: the gun

The kinetic battery crossed from one plan to the next almost unchanged, and for a reason. At about 10 km/s, penetration is hydrodynamic: it depends on rod length and the square root of the density ratio, not on velocity. Rod length is capped by launch stress. Once a navy has the densest practical alloy and the longest rod the launch allows, the gun is finished as a design problem.

The Old Forest already fired the same rounds as the Garden. The Yggdrasil and the Holzweiber carry PILVM, 5.0 kg, 645 × 21.5 mm at L/D 30, and the Holzweiber carried the 1.7 kg round until her refit.

It stayed because the two weapons do opposite jobs. The beam arrives at the speed of light and cannot be dodged, but a woven hull is a poor converter and most of the shower passes through: it kills what is inside. The rod carries more energy than any channel into a hole 21.5 mm wide, and nothing stops it: it breaks what is there. The beam blinds; the rod kills the blind.

The UREB proved its worth. It never had the penetrating and energetic power of a full kinetic round.

### 6.4 Analogues for the gun

- **The weapons that outlived their eras.** The Browning M2 entered service in 1933 and is still in use. The Bofors 40 mm dates from the 1930s and is still mounted on ships. The 5-inch/38 armed American ships from the 1930s into the 1990s. Each became mature enough to stop being a design problem, and went on everything.
- **The F-4 Phantom.** It entered service without an internal gun, because missiles were expected to make guns obsolete. Over Vietnam that proved wrong, and the F-4E was built with a cannon. The new weapon earned its place and still did not replace the old one.
- **One calibre.** In the 1830s the Royal Navy standardised its broadside armament on the 32-pounder, so every ship could use the same shot. Because the forest classes and the Garden fire the same natures, tens of thousands of older hulls and the current line draw the same rounds from the same tenders.
