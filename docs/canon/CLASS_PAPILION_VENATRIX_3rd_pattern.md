# PAPILION VENATRIX

## Class document — battlecruiser / ship-of-the-line
### Third pattern, the marksman refit.
### Reconciled against MATERIA, BOOK OF THE LOOM, TORMENTA issue 3 and TELVM issue 3.

Role: independent hunter and line-of-battle killer. The heavy element. Kills hulls. Deadliest cold.

This document states what she can do and what she was not made to do. How to use her belongs to the doctrine and the fleet list; the notes carried there are listed in Appendix A.

It supersedes the second pattern, issue 2.4, whose figures are kept in that document. The third pattern changes her aim: a shortened flexure path, more off-axis references, and rated shot preparation. Her beam, guns, hull and wings are unchanged except for the wingtip sensor trusses.

Figures marked est. are working figures, still under rating. Beam reaches are at the TELVM standard conditions: 5 nT transverse field, stack rating 0.3 µrad. Changes are listed in Appendix B.

## 1. The wings

The wings do not rotate, sweep or train. They are dorsal structural deltas on stayed spars, hinged at the root, with two positions only: spread, and folded at 20°.

There are two panels per side, in the manner of a butterfly. The forewing is larger, forward, with a straight leading edge that carries the optical battery. The hindwing is smaller, aft and raked, and carries the bulk of the mane. Each wingtip carries a small truss with radio and microwave sensors, an off-axis reference for her aim (§5).

Nothing on the ship trains except the sixteen kinetic mounts. The spinal battery is aimed by heading and trimmed ±2° magnetically. The optical battery is aimed by phase. There are no moving parts in the beam or optical armament, and that is the architectural argument of the class.

### 1.1 Why the array sits on the forewing

| Reason | |
|---|---|
| Non-collinearity | The forewing edges are 140 m apart laterally and 60 m off the spinal axis. A shower cored on one bearing does not take both. |
| Thermal | The mane is on the hindwing, one root away: the shortest path in the ship for 22.5 MW of array waste heat. |
| Metrology | Each panel holds λ/20 across its own aperture. Two such benches 140 m apart, plus the bow truss, make three stations (§5). |
| Aperture | A wing leading edge is the only long, flat, forward-facing, continuous surface on the hull. |

### 1.2 The cost

The wings are her radiators, and the mane on the hindwing extends them: it trails aft, away from the hull, sheds heat and charge together, and is covered when she is head-on. Forewing and hindwing on a side share one strong root. If it dies, that side's optical group and mane go together. This is the design and it is not to be corrected.

### 1.3 Coherence

Combination is within a panel, never across the wing. Fifty-one nanometres held across ten metres of rigid bench is a rating; across sixty metres of woven edge it is not. Twelve panels on one target add incoherently. The aperture is ten metres, never sixty. The earlier strip-lock working note is struck.

## 2. Spinal battery: 16 × 19 GeV UREB in two stacks

The spine does not train. It is aimed by heading and trimmed ±2° per channel by magnetic windage. There is no flight time.

The citadel is the weapon, and it is built as two stacks rather than sixteen separate guns. Each is a TELVM LINE stack. Stack overhead is 3.2 t/m regardless of channel count, which is why each stack carries many channels. The stacks are built stiff and aligned, and her aiming reference is carried close to them, so the path that can bend between where she measures and where the beam leaves is short.

### 2.1 Why sixteen, why two, why eight then eight

Fourteen channels at 8 kA left no room for the alley between stacks. Sixteen channels in two stacks leave a street down the middle, and the barbettes of the kinetic battery drop down it. A beam turret would punch the citadel; the split is why the ship can carry superfiring mounts at all.

The plant is sized as a one-stack store, about 1.8 GJ live, when the stacks are staggered ten seconds apart. Firing both stacks at once needs the store doubled, or the second stack fires brown. The ship can do it. It costs.

### 2.2 What the spine does to a target

The spinal beam affects a target in several distinct ways, each with its own threshold. They are set out together in §3.

The spine does not need to open a hull to disable a ship. A 19 GeV shower that reaches the interior through intact plate kills the electronics and the people in its path through bremsstrahlung and system-generated EMP. *Opened* and *mission-killed* are different results.

Woven hulls are not titanium. MAJOR-6 is a poor converter. She executes last decade's navy. A sister is a different funeral.

### 2.3 The beam arrives at the speed of light

Her spinal beam travels at the speed of light. Every other weapon in a fight crosses distance slowly enough to be answered. Hers arrives before anyone knows it was fired.

| Weapon | Range | Time to arrive | Can the target respond? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Spinal beam | 3,600 km | about 12 ms | no |
| Spinal beam | 10,000 km | about 33 ms | no |
| PILVM rod, 10 km/s | 1,000 km | 100 s | yes; dodgeable beyond about 70 km |
| Torpedo at about 30 g | 10,000 km | about 4 minutes | yes; point defence, blinding, manoeuvre |

At 10,000 km, the time from light leaving the target to the beam arriving is about 67 ms. A target accelerating at 5 m/s² moves about a centimetre in that time. The target cannot dodge, no interceptor exists, and above a few hundred MeV no armour stops the shower. What limits a hit is her own pointing, not anything the target can do.

A torpedo's range is how far it can fly. Her beam's range is how far it is certain to act.

### 2.4 Theatre

She is not built for a strong planetary well. In low orbit, inside a field of around 40 µT, the spine is a short spear. In Mars space, the interplanetary field and cislunar space, the ±2° trim covers the windage. Field-aligned geometry helps wherever it exists.

## 3. Effects by range

What she does to a target is set by two limits, and the lesser governs:

- Her beam: how far one stack can carry the fluence or dose an effect needs.
- Her aim: how far she can be sure of landing on the target. At 6.5 µrad she misses by about 6.5 m per 1,000 km. Against a line-rate citadel that is good to 9,892 km; against a whole silhouette, masts and wings included, to 14,831 km. These are her walls. Beyond a wall hits do not stop; each salvo is simply less sure of landing.

### 3.3 What sets the spot

The spot has two terms (TORMENTA §4.7).

The first is the beam's own charge. An unneutralised 6.0 kA channel spreads under its own space charge on both axes, roughly in proportion to range. The interplanetary plasma is far too thin to neutralise it. This sets the spot close in, and its width at every range.

The second is the ambient field. A 19 GeV electron has a magnetic rigidity of about 63 T·m. In an interplanetary field of about 5 nT its path curves with a radius of about 12.7 million km, bending the beam sideways by about 350 m over 3,000 km. That systematic bend is what the ±2° trim and the probe barrel correct. Electrons of slightly different energies bend by slightly different amounts, so the beam fans out into a streak along the bend, growing with the square of range. This sets the spot's length beyond about 1,750 km at Mk.2 (about 700 km at Mk.1).

Particle energy is bounded by the citadel: at 100 MeV/m, 38 GeV needs 380 m of accelerator. It is a question of gradient for a later pattern. Raising particle energy adds no charge; raising current does, and tends to widen the energy spread.

### 3.4 Braking range

Bremsstrahlung means braking radiation. It is how most of the shower's energy crosses a hull that is not opened.

Lethal doses are very small beside the energy needed to open a hull. Opening a woven hull takes around 83 MJ/m². About 5 Gy kills within weeks without treatment; some tens of gray incapacitate within minutes to hours. If about a third of the shower energy crossing a person is absorbed, and a body presents about 250 kg per square metre, one gray takes roughly 750 J/m², and 50 Gy about 0.04 MJ/m².

A single Mk.2 channel delivers 50 Gy out to about 26,900 km, and 5 Gy to about 56,300 km, well beyond her walls of 9,892 km for a line citadel and 14,831 km for a full silhouette. Braking range is set by where she can land the beam, not by the beam's energy.

A spinal hit on an occupied volume kills the people in it at every range at which she can aim, whether or not the hull is opened. Unhardened electronics fail at far lower doses and die the same way. De-masting is the only spinal effect that does not reach the crew, because a mast is external structure.

### 3.5 Shattering

A beam deposits its energy in well under a microsecond. In titanium or steel plate that sudden heating drives a stress wave, and if the stress exceeds the plate's spall strength, the inner face tears away and is thrown into the interior as fragments. The hull is shattered rather than holed. The model takes the titanium shatter threshold at about half its opening fluence, est., which puts shattering about a quarter further out than opening: 5,530 km against 4,310 km. The fragments are lethal to anyone behind the plate.

### 4.3 Point defence, against missiles only

A missile is bright, isolated and hot, so the tracking loop closes on it and open-loop pointing does not enter. Twelve tracks retarget in microseconds against a weapon that takes minutes to arrive. Missile tankage around 3 mm thick needs about 30 MJ/m² as a working kill fluence, est..

| Range | Dwell, one panel | Kills per second, twelve panels |
|---|---|---|
| 300 km | 0.087 s | ~140 |
| 1,000 km | 0.97 s | ~12 |
| 3,000 km | 8.7 s | ~1.4 |
| 10,000 km | 96 s | 0.13 |

It cannot touch a round. A cold inert rod 21.5 mm across subtends 54 nanoradians at 400 km and radiates nothing, so there is no loop to close. The battery cannot track a rod or kill one at any range. That is why the fleet fires rods and not missiles.

### 4.4 Sensor suppression

An enemy aperture looking at her retroreflects off its own focal plane, which locates it to a fraction of a microradian. Focal planes fail at microjoules. The envelope is any open aperture out to about 40,000 km. Light lag there is 133 ms and never binds.

The limit is that it works only on an open aperture. A shutter closes in microseconds, and dazzle recovers in seconds. She taxes a competent enemy rather than blinding him. Shutter discipline costs an enemy array 30–40% of its integration time, degrading his pointing by roughly a quarter (15 µrad to about 19) and pulling his citadel wall in by around 800 km, est..

Optical dazzle is temporary and defeated by a shutter. Spinal de-masting is permanent and defeated by nothing. They are different effects.

### 4.5 Fire control

At 0.126 µrad a panel resolves 5 cm at 400 km. TEREBRA is unguided because she can watch where the last round landed and walk the pattern. The first pattern did this with the bow truss at 15 µrad, 6 m at 400 km, barely inside the 18 m CEP it was correcting. The arrays improve fall-of-shot resolution by about 120 times, and the terrestrial fit in §6 is usable only because of them.

At low duty the same glass serves as lidar, tracker and probe.

### 4.6 Seeker interference

Active illumination into an optical seeker at any of the irradiances in §4.2 saturates it. It is ineffective against inertial guidance and entirely ineffective against a rod.

### 4.7 Not an anti-ship weapon

Twelve panels deliver 372 MW/m² at 1,000 km, and a woven hull opens at about 83 MJ/m², est., which suggests a dwell of 0.22 s. That figure is false, because of the mount. The spot at 1,000 km is 0.25 m across; open-loop pointing error is 6.5 µrad, or 6.5 m. The spot is about 26 times smaller than the aim error, so an open-loop beam wanders and dilutes its own irradiance by around 680 times. The true dwell to open a hull at 1,000 km is about two and a half minutes. No power increase corrects this. The limit is the mount, not the optics.

A round arrives with about 43 kg TNT equivalent. Against three metres of bunker roof that is everything. Against a city it is nothing. She is a chisel, not a hammer.

## 7. Protection

| Zone | Coverage | Areal density |
|---|---|---|
| Citadel | 26% | 547 kg/m² |
| Joints, barbettes, rings | 4% | 688 kg/m² |
| Bumper and scale array | 60% | 18 kg/m² |
| Bare weave: apertures, sensor roots, panel benches | 10% | |

What the armour does: it defeats spall, contains fire, absorbs beam fluence below breach, and can be re-woven at range.

What it does not do is stop a beam. 547 kg/m² is about 1.3 radiation lengths, and 95% containment of a 19 GeV shower needs about eighteen. The citadel absorbs under 2% of a sister's shower. Above a few hundred MeV, nothing any ship carries is a shield.

Against a rod, 547 kg/m² defeats 9.3 cm of penetrator. Against a 64.5 cm PILVM it is a fire boundary and a spall trap, not a stop.

Obliquity is the one free improvement and the class does not claim it. At 20° incidence, 547 kg/m² presents 1,453 kg/m², 2.7 times as much, which takes rod defeat from 9.3 cm to 25 cm. No PAPILION has ever been classed on her worst-bearing angle. The Shell Chamber objection of 2413 on the bare ten per cent stands beside that decision, unanswered.

The panel benches are bare weave over Branch D structure and are counted in the ten per cent. A panel is not armoured and cannot be.

Doctrine stands in for armour. She dies to herself: same grammar, same hollow 70 kt, same 2.3 m of fibre in front of a sister's needle.

## 8. Thermal, endurance and the mane

| | |
|---|---|
| Thermal store | 10.8 TJ |
| Quiet load, wings spread | 100 MW |

| Wings spread | the clock does not run |

### 8.1 The mane is the electrical earth

Firing drives electrons out; the hull charges positive, and the whiskers bleed the charge away as ions.

| | One stack | Both stacks |
|---|---|---|
| Charge | 38.4 mC | 76.8 mC |
| Hull capacitance, half-beam ~45 m | ~5 nF | ~5 nF |
| Potential if unbled | ~7.7 MV | ~15 MV |
| Stored energy if unbled | ~150 kJ | ~590 kJ |

Emission is contact ionisation of an alkali on a high-work-function whisker: osmium at 5.93 eV against caesium at 3.89 eV. Consumption is about 0.05 mg of caesium per stack. The alkali has never been the constraint.

### 8.2 The constraint is exposure

The mane is welded, not woven. She cannot replace it from her own stores: she carries a loom, not a wire-works. Its whiskers are made to be detached and replaced, and fleet tenders replace them at range. A tender also takes her hot coolant at range and gives her cold, moving the heat onto its own radiator wings.

It is high-Z, unarmoured, hot when working, and trails aft of the hindwings.

She cannot fire the spine twice without opening the mane, and an open mane is visible. The first shot is free; the second is not.

With wings spread, the clock does not run and the mane is shown. She cannot be patient and covered at once.

Killing the mane is a mission kill at a fraction of the energy needed to open her hull, against a target larger and softer than a mast. It is not in her threat set. It should be.

A TOW-C graphite conductor mesh woven through the shell, as a distributed cold emitter, is the only known route that moves her rate of fire off two unarmoured welded structures. It is not fitted in the third pattern, and it remains the standing recommendation.

## 9. Forward truss and stores

The 100 m truss carries the main sensor arrays and bulk auxiliaries. It is unarmoured wireframe, killable from more than twice the range at which her hull can be opened, and it is used as cheap volume: mass carried there is mass nobody has to armour.

| | Forward truss | Citadel |
|---|---|---|
| Ready-use rounds | 20% | 80% |
| Splice stores: ACIN-3, PIRI, AUX-1 | 20% | 80% |
| Dope casks | none | all |

The first pattern held the splice stores forward. The truss is the thing most likely to die, and it was holding what the ship needs after it dies. The second pattern reverses the split, so a de-masted PAPILION still has her repair stores. Dope casks, cold hold, cask plant and loom are citadel fittings without exception.

## 10. What she can do, and what she was not made to do

### She can

Her capabilities, whether or not her role calls on them.

Against ships:

- Open titanium hulls at 4,310 km and woven hulls at 3,600 km with one stack on one spot, and shatter titanium plate at 5,530 km. Against last decade's navy she wins an independent action.
- Open a woven hull with a single Mk.2 channel at 2,230 km, outside the Mk.1 annihilation floor.
- Lay one stack as a single spot of eight channels, or divide it across up to eight targets; two stacks give two patterns.
- Kill the crew and electronics of any struck volume at every range at which she can aim: 9,892 km against a line citadel, further against larger ones.
- Strike at the speed of light. Nothing she fires at can dodge the beam or intercept it.
- Take masts at 11,600 km, and radiators and manes at 14,831 km.
- Mission-kill plated ships with the spine and finish them with PILVM.
- Bring all sixteen guns to bear in a full broadside of 4.00 GJ.
- Dazzle any open aperture out to about 40,000 km.
- Kill about twelve missiles a second at 1,000 km with no moving parts.

Against a fixed installation, which cannot dodge:

- Put PILVM into it at any range she can aim, with hits approaching certainty, 2,500 km and beyond.
- Bore it with TEREBRA: 3.26 m per salvo, 196 m from the standard fit, 1,724 m from the barrage fit; sixteen guns at 2.88 GJ on target in one bore salvo.
- Resolve her own fall of shot to 5 cm at 400 km, and walk the pattern onto a hundred-metre target.
- Take its masts at 11,600 km and its radiators at 14,831 km, and irradiate its crew to her citadel wall.

Alone:

- Hunt and kill on her own inside a star system as an independent hunter.
- Cruise indefinitely with her wings spread: the clock does not run.
- Lie cold for 36 hours with her wings folded, 28.4 hours with the optical battery firing.
- Fire one stack salvo every 10 s for about 62 hours from her 10.8 TJ store, est.
- Carry a campaign magazine: 28,200 rounds in the barrage fit, about 1,760 full broadsides.
- Repair at range: she carries a loom and a splice party, and her splice stores sit in the citadel.

Measuring and taking damage:

- Aim to 6.5 µrad cued, from her truss, forewing groups and wingtip trusses, with every shot prepared.
- Fight de-masted at 18 µrad, keeping her mast reach to 5,356 km.
- Lose a wing root and keep the other side's optical group and her whole spine and gun battery.

### She was not made to

These are outside what she was built for, and other hulls or other fits do them.

- Use the spine as a long gun inside a strong planetary well. At 40 µT it opens woven at 200 km; her guns work there.
- Mass both stacks on one rated spot. The stacks are not rated against each other; she lays two patterns.
- Extend her braking range with energy. Pointing extends it.
- Combine twelve panels into a sixty-metre aperture. Each panel is a ten-metre aperture.
- Track a rod.
- Open a hull with light. Her light dazzles, ranges, directs her fire and kills missiles.
- Strike an occupied volume without killing its occupants. Every spinal hit on one does.
- Turn and shoot at once. A lateral dodge costs seven seconds of main armament; translation is free, rotation is not.
- Fight two of her own kind. One on one against an equal, the annihilation floor sits at about 2,400 km.
- Fire her whole magazine at a fleet on the standard fit. A third of it is entry rounds; the fleet-action fit carries none.
- Fight and dig at once. A full forward broadside pitches her and walks the spine off its target.
- Be patient and covered at once. Spread, the clock stops and her mane shows.
- Cross the abyss on her own drive. That is the flower, or the tow.
- Run long picket. Her cold clock is 36 hours.
- Stand over a gate as AEGIS. That is AESHNA.

## Appendix A. Notes carried to doctrine

These belong in the doctrine and the fleet list, not the class particulars. They are kept here so nothing is lost between documents.

- Station her behind the frigate screen, working the second rank in the suppression band.
- Fire a probe barrel, then an offset stack. Do not spend eight identical windages.
- One stack, then the other; firing both at once is a surge, not a standard.
- Plan the second spinal shot, which opens the mane.
- Fire beams and guns together. Never guns first.
- Roll under beam fire even with nothing to bring to bear; it changes which internal lines sit on a given shower.
- Concentrate to open; spread to cover.
- Report dazzle, de-mast, shattered, opened and mission-killed separately.
- Declare the magazine split in the fleet list before sailing.
- Do not expose the mast because losing it now costs less.
- An AESHNA, if allocated, is how the weather dies without her having to glow.
- She is a battlecruiser and a ship-of-the-line, not a battleship.
