# PHALAENA SPECULATRIX

## Class document: frigate

Third pattern: the four-stack refit.
Reconciled against MATERIA, BOOK OF THE LOOM, TORMENTA issue 3 and TELVM issue 3, 30,000 series. Supersedes the second-pattern document entire; its figures are kept in Appendix C.

Role: picket, scout and blinding weapon. Opens the action by taking eyes, and opens hulls when she closes. Against classic titanium she decides it.

Figures marked *est.* are estimates, not ratings. Beam reaches are at the TELVM standard conditions: 5 nT transverse field, stack rating 0.3 µrad.

## 1. The wings

The wings do not rotate, sweep or train. One dorsal delta per side on stayed spars, hinged at the root, with two positions: spread, and folded 20°.

| Edge | Carries |
|---|---|
| Leading edge | the optical strip |
| Back half: trailing edge and the area around it | the mane |

The wings are her radiators. Each wing is joined to the hull at one root per side, and the root is strong. The strip on the leading edge, facing forward so it can see, and the mane over the back half of the wing belong to the wing, not the root: the mane extends the radiator, sheds heat and charge together, trails behind her, and is covered by the wing when she is head-on. If a root dies, that wing, its strip and its mane go together. This is the design and it is not to be corrected.

1.1 Why the strip is on the wing and not on the truss. The truss is open wireframe carrying twenty-four beam apertures at its root, and it is the most killable structure on the ship. The wing edges are 80 m apart laterally and off the spinal axis. This was the class's first non-collinear installation of anything.

### 2.2 What one stack does, all six on one spot

What she does to a target is set by two limits, and the lesser governs:

- Her beam: how far one stack carries the fluence or dose an effect needs.
- Her aim: how far she can be sure of landing on the target. At 5.5 µrad she misses by about 5.5 m per 1,000 km. Against a line-rate citadel that is good to 11,691 km; against a whole silhouette, masts and wings included, to 17,527 km. These are her walls. Beyond a wall hits do not stop; each salvo is simply less sure of landing.

### 2.7 Why thread, and why Mk.2

A 150 m citadel holds 15 GeV at the standard gradient, and no lever but length raises it.

FILVM is the lightest channel on charge and on mass: a four-stack salvo charges her hull to 7.6 MV, and one stack to 1.9 MV. A mast is ruined by 2.91 MJ/m² where a hull needs 82.7, a factor of 28. A weak beam takes an eye exactly as well as a strong one. Titanium needs 50.7 MJ/m² where woven needs 82.7, so she opens a titanium hull 350 km further than a woven one, and shatters titanium plate at 2,490 km.

Mk.2 is worth 13% in hull-opening reach and 24% in mast reach over Mk.1 on this stack (1,370 km and 4,410 km at Mk.1), at no cost in current, charge, pulse energy or mane charge.

### 3.4 What the strip does

3.4.1 Sensor suppression from the cold state. An enemy aperture standing open retroreflects off its own focal plane, which locates it to a fraction of a microradian and makes it a cooperative target.

The optical strip is the only weapon on this ship that does not open her mane. Firing all four stacks charges the hull to 7.6 MV, and one stack to 1.9 MV, and the whiskers must open to shed it. An open mane is a light. The strip charges nothing. It costs 5.62 MW of heat and 7.8 hours of the cold clock, and nothing else.

A picket can therefore suppress an enemy's sensors from cold, on station, without breaking cold. The honest limit: the ship she lases knows she is there, and knows the bearing. At 0.251 µrad the beam is 5 m across at 10,000 km, so she is detected by her target and by no one else. She has not broken cold. She has told one ship.

Envelope: any aperture standing open, out to 40,000 km. Light lag at that range is 133 ms against a 10 s cycle.

Effect is a tax, not a kill. A shutter closes in microseconds and dazzle recovers in seconds. Shutter discipline costs an enemy array 30–40% of its integration time, which degrades his pointing by roughly a quarter, est.

This class has three blindings, and a signal that says "blinded" without saying which is useless:

| Mechanism | Range | Character |
|---|---|---|
| Optical dazzle | 40,000 km | temporary; shutter-defeated; does not break cold |
| Beam mast-kill | 5,450 km per stack, four at once | permanent; nothing defeats it; opens the mane |
| Guns on a blinded target | approach march | terminal |

3.4.2 Active ranging. PANEL II against a dark hull at 5,000 km:

| | |
|---|---|
| Transmitted | 384 kW |
| Return | diffuse into 2π, 5% albedo |
| Collected by a 19.6 m² aperture | 2.4 nW |
| Photons per nanosecond gate | ~12 |
| Range accuracy | 0.15 m at 1 ns timing |

Convergence fire requires range to about 44 m at 3,322 km. A signal-capital pair at a 2,000 km baseline gives 32 m. A single-formation parallax gives 736 m. A picket with an active strip gives 0.15 m inside 5,000 km, which covers the whole of her hull-opening band and nearly all of her mast band.

The fleet standing order requires a fallback convergence baseline to be named in the fleet list before the action, not chosen during it. A picket squadron carrying strips is that fallback, and it needs no baseline: it measures range directly.

Its cost is that it is an emission. A ranging strip is not a quiet ship, and ranging and picket are exclusive states.

3.4.3 Point defence, secondary on this class. Missile tankage at 3 mm needs ~30 MJ/m².

| Range | Dwell, one panel | Kills per second, twelve panels |
|---|---|---|
| 300 km | 1.38 s | ~8.7 |
| 1,000 km | 15.3 s | ~0.8 |
| 3,000 km | 140 s | 0.09 |

Her strip clears a leaker and does not break up a salvo.

3.4.4 What it cannot touch. A cold inert rod, 15.0 mm across, is 38 nanoradians at 400 km and radiates nothing. There is no loop to close. The strip cannot track a round and cannot kill one, at any range.

And it cannot open a hull. Spot at 1,000 km is 0.50 m against 5.5 µrad of open-loop aim scatter, 5.5 m. The beam wanders and dilutes itself by (error/spot)², a factor near 120. True dwell to open a hull at 1,000 km is about seven minutes.

### 4.1 The stations

|---|---|---|

| Starboard wing strip | 40 m off axis | as above |
| Lateral separation | 80 m | |
| Backup | citadel stub, 40 µrad | inside 1,600 km |

Her aim rests on three things:

- A flexure path near zero. The baseline sets how well she measures, and she wants it long; the flexure path from reference to muzzle sets how well she aims, and she wants it zero. Ringing the apertures at the truss root gives her that.
- Three stations. The truss and the two wing strips measure each other's bending, and it is subtracted.
- Shot preparation. Under the MINERVA programme every crew is trained and rated to prepare the shot as a marksman does: the hull quieted, the stacks settled, a probe shot fired and the trim corrected before the salvo. It is standard drill, and her rating assumes it.

The truss root remains the single worst concentration on the ship: twenty-four apertures, the main arrays and the aiming reference, on one bearing. It cannot be moved without moving the flexure path, and moving the flexure path costs her the pointing that makes her a frigate. The class accepts this and buys its way out with redundancy instead of layout. Four stacks add redundancy the single stack did not have: a hit that takes one stack's channels leaves three patterns.

## 8. What she can do

Her capabilities, whether or not her role calls on them.

### 8.1 Against ships

- Open titanium hulls at 1,900 km, four at once, one per stack, every 10 seconds. Against a classic titanium navy she decides battles.
- Shatter titanium plate at 2,490 km.
- Open woven hulls at 1,550 km.
- Take sensor masts at 5,450 km, four at once, one per stack.
- Ruin radiators at 7,370 km and manes at 8,450 km.
- Irradiate the crew and electronics of any citadel she can aim at: 11,691 km against a line citadel, further against larger ones.
- Lay up to 24 separate spots in one salvo, in four independent patterns.
- Suppress an enemy's open apertures from 40,000 km without shedding charge, opening her mane, or breaking cold.
- Finish a blinded target with eight guns at ranges no dodging ship could be hit at.
- Defeat a 900 kg/m² titanium belt with eighty per cent of her rod to spare.
- Clear missile leakers: about 8.7 a second at 300 km.

### 8.2 Against a fixed installation

A station, a base or a skyhook cannot dodge. Everything she carries reaches it.

- Take its masts at 5,450 km, four at once, and its radiators at 7,370 km.
- Irradiate its crew and electronics to her citadel wall, 11,691 km against a line-rate citadel, further against a larger structure.
- Put rods into it at any range she can aim: about 30 m of aim at 5,450 km and 64 m at 11,691 km, where a round flies about 19 minutes.
- Defeat any belt it carries: 1.64 m of woven, 265 g/cm².
- Keep up 1.36 GJ of rounds every 10 s for about 6 hours on her own magazine: 35,000 rounds, 2.98 TJ delivered.
- Dodge its rods outside about 47 km while it cannot dodge hers.

### 8.3 Alone and cold

- Cruise the Astrum alone, as picket or police, for as long as her stores carry her.
- Cruise indefinitely on her heat with her wings spread. Spread, her wings shed her 35 MW quiet load and the thermal clock does not run.
- Lie dark for 48 hours at a stretch, wings folded and strip cold; 40.2 hours with the strip working.
- Fire from her own store without a tender: about 66 hours of four-stack salvos, about 11 days of one-stack salvos, or about 12 hours of all eight guns, from 5.0 TJ.
- Fire for a very long time on her mane: 0.029 mg of alkali per four-stack salvo.
- Accelerate at about 6.16 m/s², est.

### 8.4 Measuring

- Measure range to 0.15 m inside 5,000 km, and stand as a fleet's fallback convergence baseline without a parallax pair.
- Provide the second parallax axis a battle line cannot generate from a single column.
- Aim to 5.5 µrad cued from three stations 80 m apart, with every shot prepared.

### 8.5 Taking damage

- Fight de-masted at 15 µrad with her full hull-opening and mast reach.
- Lose a stack and keep three patterns.
- Patch weave damage herself with her damage-control weaving.
- Lose a wing and keep half her optical battery, all four stacks and all eight guns. De-masted as well, she still aims from the stub to 1,600 km.

## 9. What she was not made to do

She was made to open an action and take eyes. These are outside that, and other hulls do them.

- Open hulls at long range. Her stacks are picket stacks: titanium at 1,900 km, woven at 1,550 km.
- Mass her battery on one rated spot. Stacks are not rated against each other; she lays four patterns instead.
- Stand in a line and take hits. Her protection is range and aim, and her citadel is a fire boundary, not armour. It does not stop a beam reaching her crew.
- Open a hull with light. Seven minutes of dwell at 1,000 km; the limit is the mount.
- Track a rod. 38 nanoradians and dark.
- Break up a missile salvo. Her strip clears leakers.
- Range and lie quiet at once. Active ranging is an emission; she chooses one state or the other.
- Fully re-weave her hull at range. That is a splice party's work; her own damage-control weaving makes partial repairs.
- Replace her own whiskers. The mane is welded; tenders replace whiskers at range.

## Appendix A. Notes for doctrine, carried for transfer

These are usage, not capability, and belong to doctrine. They are carried here updated until doctrine takes them.

A.1 She opens the action, and she can decide it. Her mast reach creates a band of roughly 5,450 to 1,900 km in which she takes eyes and does not yet open hulls. Inside 1,900 km she opens titanium hulls four at a time, every 10 seconds; inside 1,550 km, woven. Crews inside it are not safe: every channel that lands irradiates the volume it strikes. The enemy arrives in the killing band already degraded.

A.2 The strip extends that band to 40,000 km and changes its character. Optical suppression is a tax, not a kill; it forces shutter discipline and costs an enemy his integration time. Begin it early, from cold, and do not report it as blinding.

A.3 Name the ranging state before the action. A squadron ranging actively is a squadron that has been located. A squadron on picket is a squadron with 736 m of parallax range. The two cannot be held at once, and the choice belongs to the commander, not to a captain.

A.4 In a fleet action, pair her. A frigate detached into an action without a heavy behind her blinds an enemy and then watches it. Detach in twos so a de-masted hull can be cross-cued; a de-masted hull can cue herself to 4,287 km, which makes the pair a matter of redundancy rather than survival. Alone, she cruises and reconnoitres on her own.

A.5 Fire beam and guns together. Never the guns first: the muzzle flash travels at light speed and precedes the rounds by up to nine minutes at her mast range.

A.6 Her guns are a capital threat. At the standard rod length she defeats every belt in existence. Inside 47 km she is a killing ship and not a scouting one, and she should not be there.

A.7 Four patterns are four targets. One salvo can take four masts, or blind one ship and irradiate three.

A.8 She is the ship that wins a battle. She is not the ship that fights a war. Do not confuse the two when composing a line.
